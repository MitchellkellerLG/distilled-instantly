## Topic: List Building & Targeting

## Overview
Instantly teaches that list building starts with a clear, evidence-based ICP and improves through ongoing refinement. Find prospects by fit, then use relevant buying signals to judge timing, verify and refresh contact data, and segment or prioritize leads so outreach reaches the right people with relevant context.

## Define and refine your ICP
Instantly recommends building an ICP from the traits of strong existing customers, then refining it with evidence from campaigns. Include negative profiles to clarify who is unlikely to be a fit.

> "The best way to build an ICP is to base it on your best existing customers + real data. Then, you refine it with firmographics, problems, buying triggers, and negative profiles, and continue to update it as campaigns run."
>
> **Source:** [link](https://instantly.ai/blog/lead-generation-guide/) · blog, 2025-12-26

> "A good ICP is as much about who you don’t want as it is about who you do."
>
> **Source:** [link](https://instantly.ai/blog/lead-generation-guide/) · blog, 2025-12-26

## Find accounts by fit and timing
Instantly teaches that ICP fit helps identify relevant prospects, while triggers and signals help determine when they may be worth contacting. Use signals tied to what you sell, rather than treating every company update as a reason to reach out.

> "Static ICP data captures fit, not timing. Imagine a company that perfectly matches your ICP but has no active buying trigger. It may not convert for six months."
>
> **Source:** [link](https://instantly.ai/blog/ai-bdr-lead-qualification/) · blog, 2026-07-13

> "The signal should be relevant to what you’re selling. Rather than treating every company update as a reason to reach out, use it as additional context when deciding whether a potential decision maker is worth contacting now."
>
> **Source:** [link](https://instantly.ai/blog/how-to-find-decision-makers-at-companies/) · blog, 2026-09-09

## Use filters and find the buying group
Instantly's March 19, 2026 lesson describes filtering leads by criteria such as title, company size, industry, location, and technology. For decision makers, check role and buying authority; larger purchases may involve several stakeholders.

> "You can filter by job title, company size, industry location, technology stack, whatever criteria matter to your ICP. Let's say you're targeting SaaS founders in the US with companies between ten and fifty employees."
>
> **Source:** [link](https://instantly.ai/get-started-with-instantly/lesson-3) · academy, modified 2026-03-19

> "Job titles are a useful starting point, but they don’t always tell you who has buying authority. Larger companies may also have several people involved in a purchase, from the person evaluating a solution to the person responsible for approving the budget."
>
> **Source:** [link](https://instantly.ai/blog/how-to-find-decision-makers-at-companies/) · blog, 2026-09-09

## Verify and refresh contact data
Instantly teaches that deliverability and role relevance are separate parts of list quality: a valid address may still be stale. Keep data quality as an ongoing operation and refresh records over time.

> "Freshness answers a different question than accuracy. An email address can be technically deliverable but belong to someone who moved into a completely different role six months ago."
>
> **Source:** [link](https://instantly.ai/blog/email-list-accuracy-freshness-tradeoffs/) · blog, 2026-04-22

> "The core principle across every step of this system is the same: treat list quality as a continuous operation, not a one-time purchase."
>
> **Source:** [link](https://instantly.ai/blog/email-list-accuracy-freshness-tradeoffs/) · blog, 2026-04-22

## Segment and prioritize prospects
Instantly recommends using the data that helps determine fit or whether a prospect is worth contacting now. Qualification establishes whether to pursue a prospect; prioritization helps decide who should receive attention first.

> "You don’t need every possible data point. Focus on information that has some bearing on whether the prospect is a good fit or worth contacting now."
>
> **Source:** [link](https://instantly.ai/blog/prioritize-sales-leads-with-ai/) · blog, 2026-09-25

> "Lead qualification determines whether a prospect is worth pursuing. Lead prioritization helps decide which of those prospects should receive attention first."
>
> **Source:** [link](https://instantly.ai/blog/prioritize-sales-leads-with-ai/) · blog, 2026-09-25

## Build lookalikes and keep targeting active
Instantly presents targeting as a system to revisit as customer and campaign data changes. Use successful customers to identify shared traits and find similar prospects.

> "A lookalike audience is built by taking one success story and asking who else looks like this."
>
> **Source:** [link](https://instantly.ai/targeting-lectures/targeting-and-enrichment-4) · academy-video, modified 2026-02-17

> "Over time, what you build isn't just a list. It's an engine of relevance, constantly refreshing itself, constantly feeding you the right accounts at the right time."
>
> **Source:** [link](https://instantly.ai/targeting-lectures/targeting-and-enrichment-6) · academy-video, modified 2026-01-15

## Topic: Copywriting & Offers

## Overview
Instantly teaches that cold email copy should quickly establish relevance, show a specific benefit, and make the next step easy. Strong openers and subject lines reflect the recipient’s situation, while credible offers reduce the effort and risk of replying.

## Subject lines that set up the message
Instantly teaches that subject lines should be concise and relevant to the recipient. The opening line should deliver on the subject line’s promise.

> "Character count matters, but relevance beats length."
>
> **Source:** [link](https://instantly.ai/blog/subject-line-copywriting-for-decision-makers-ctos-vs-cfos-vs-founders/) · blog, 2026-03-23

> "Subject line and greeting alignment is the strategic consistency between the promise made in your subject line and the payoff in your first line."
>
> **Source:** [link](https://instantly.ai/blog/subject-line-greeting-synergy-how-to-align-your-email-opener-with-your-subject-line/) · blog, 2026-03-30

## Open with relevance
Instantly teaches that an opener should show the prospect you understand their situation. Specific, personalized details can help earn attention.

> "Novel, specific, or personalized openers bypass the filter and capture attention."
>
> **Source:** [link](https://instantly.ai/blog/how-to-write-email-openers-that-stand-out-7-psychological-tactics/) · blog, 2026-03-30

> "Your opening line has one job: make the prospect think "this person understands my situation." Three tactics that get you there:"
>
> **Source:** [link](https://instantly.ai/blog/proposal-email-body-structure-framework/) · blog, 2026-04-20

## Lead with the recipient’s outcome
Instantly teaches that copy should focus on the prospect’s needs and the outcome they could get, rather than starting with a pitch about your company.

> "Lead with a likely outcome or pain solved. In controlled tests, clarity and specificity beat persuasion."
>
> **Source:** [link](https://instantly.ai/blog/train-sdrs-high-performing-subject-lines/) · blog, 2025-10-14

> "WIIFM (What's In It For Me) is the lens through which every recipient reads your email. People are motivated by self-interest."
>
> **Source:** [link](https://instantly.ai/blog/how-to-write-email-openers-that-stand-out-7-psychological-tactics/) · blog, 2026-03-30

## Make the offer concrete and credible
Instantly teaches that a useful offer gives prospects something relevant they can use, and that reducing perceived risk can help build trust.

> "You lead with what you can give. And this isn't a PDF, it's not a Loom video, it's not a hop on a call. It's an actual deliverable, something that they can actually use."
>
> **Source:** [watch at 0:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7gSLBPqosZ0&t=44s) · youtube, 2026-03-19

> "What you need to do is you need to identify a specific problem in the prospect's business. And in the offer, you offer to fix the first part of that problem for free with no strings attached."
>
> **Source:** [watch at 0:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7gSLBPqosZ0&t=51s) · youtube, 2026-03-19

## Keep the copy focused
Instantly teaches that each sentence should earn its place by building relevance, describing an outcome, or prompting a response.

> "Every sentence in a proposal email should serve one of three jobs: establish relevance, state a specific outcome, or prompt a reply. If a sentence does neither, cut it."
>
> **Source:** [link](https://instantly.ai/blog/proposal-email-length-and-format-how-long-should-your-email-be-and-how-to-structure-it-visually/) · blog, 2026-04-13

## Ask for an easy reply
Instantly teaches that cold email CTAs should make replying simple. Asking for a meeting or demo immediately can create more friction than an interest-based, low-effort next step.

> "Trying to schedule a meeting or book a demo on the first email is a big ask. Instead, use interest-based low-effort CTAs. The goal here is to get your prospects to reply."
>
> **Source:** [link](https://instantly.ai/blog/prospecting-email-templates/) · blog, 2023-11-06

> "Make your first offer something that they can say yes to in around 10 seconds without getting on a call."
>
> **Source:** [watch at 9:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dg2xNFyD6ng&t=587s) · youtube, 2025-11-28

## Topic: Personalization & AI

## Overview
Instantly teaches that effective personalization starts with relevant prospect context—such as role, company signals, and pain points—and connects that context to the offer. AI and automation can help research, enrich, and draft at scale, while accurate inputs, clear constraints, and human review help keep messages useful and grounded.

## Relevance over surface details
Instantly teaches that personalization should show an understanding of the prospect’s situation, not just repeat information like their name or company.

> "Prospects do not care that you know their first name and company. They care that you understand their situation."
>
> **Source:** [link](https://instantly.ai/targeting-lectures/targeting-and-enrichment-5) · academy, modified 2026-02-17

> "The most durable personalization layer is role-level relevance. Understanding what someone actually does day-to-day, not just their job title, lets you map their primary frustration to the outcome your proposal delivers."
>
> **Source:** [link](https://instantly.ai/blog/proposal-email-personalization-mechanisms/) · blog, 2026-04-20

## Research tied to the offer
Instantly teaches that research is useful when it helps explain why the offer matters to that prospect. Leave out details that are unrelated or cannot be supported confidently.

> "The research should still have a clear connection to what you’re offering. Mentioning an unrelated company announcement may make an email look personalized without making it more relevant."
>
> **Source:** [link](https://instantly.ai/blog/automate-prospect-research-with-ai/) · blog, 2026-09-15

> "If a claim about the prospect or their company can’t be supported confidently, it’s better to leave it out than build personalization around an assumption."
>
> **Source:** [link](https://instantly.ai/blog/automate-prospect-research-with-ai/) · blog, 2026-09-15

## Building personalization at scale
Instantly teaches that reusable systems can map prospect signals to relevant content blocks, making messages feel tailored without requiring a unique email for every contact.

> "The solution is architectural. You build a system that pulls the right signals, maps them to the right content blocks, and assembles a custom-feeling email automatically."
>
> **Source:** [link](https://instantly.ai/blog/proposal-email-personalization-mechanisms/) · blog, 2026-04-20

> "For all the talk of personalization, it’s unlikely that you’re actually going to write a unique email per customer. That’s simply unachievable, especially at scale. Instead, you should seek to create emails for segments of your audience, and then use your email software to send these emails as one campaign."
>
> **Source:** [link](https://instantly.ai/blog/targeted-email-marketing/) · blog, 2023-02-27

## AI as a copilot
Instantly teaches that AI can support drafting and repetitive work, while people guide strategy, brand voice, and judgment. Its April 20, 2026 proposal-email guidance describes a human approval step before sending.

> "The founders who get this wrong treat AI as an autopilot. The ones who book meetings treat it as a co-pilot: AI handles the first draft and the scaling busywork, while you inject the strategic positioning, objection handling, and brand voice that actually converts."
>
> **Source:** [link](https://instantly.ai/blog/ai-proposal-email-writing-limitations/) · blog, 2026-04-20

> "The most productive use of AI in proposal email workflows is Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) mode: AI drafts, you approve, then it sends."
>
> **Source:** [link](https://instantly.ai/blog/ai-proposal-email-writing-limitations/) · blog, 2026-04-20

## Prompting and quality checks
Instantly teaches that prompts should define the audience and constrain the model to reliable inputs. Sample review helps catch weak or inaccurate output before a campaign goes live.

> "Instruct the AI to use only these fields and never invent facts."
>
> **Source:** [link](https://instantly.ai/blog/ai-powered-cold-email-personalization-safe-patterns-prompt-examples-workflow-for-founders/) · blog, 2025-09-04

> "The workflow runs in four steps: 1. **Pull contacts:** Pull verified contacts from your lead source with enrichment fields (role, company size, industry, recent trigger event) 2. **Map variables:** Map those fields to AI prompt variables such as {{RecentHire}} and {{CompanyIndustry}} 3. **Generate at scale:** Run the AI prompt across the list to produce unique opening lines for each contact 4. **Sample review:** Review a subset of outputs for quality before launching to the full list"
>
> **Source:** [link](https://instantly.ai/blog/ai-proposal-email-writing-limitations/) · blog, 2026-04-20

## Topic: Sequences & Follow-Ups

## Overview
Instantly teaches that outreach sequences work best as planned, measurable touchpoints, with clear timing, a distinct purpose for each message, and rules for when to stop. Follow-ups should add fresh value and adapt to prospect responses and buying signals. Treat example cadences as starting points, then use results to refine timing and content.

## Plan each step and its exit rule
Give each message a purpose and a clear place in the sequence. Set exit rules so a reply, booking, or opt-out stops automated outreach.

> "Each step has a set day, a set goal, and an exit rule: the moment a prospect replies, books, or opts out, the sequence stops."
>
> **Source:** [link](https://instantly.ai/blog/sales-email-sequence-examples/) · blog, 2024-03-06

## Set a deliberate cadence
Cadence covers the intervals, daily caps, and sending days that shape an outreach sequence. Instantly’s March 2026 guidance suggests spacing early follow-ups three to seven days apart, then widening the gaps later.

> ""Cadence" describes the structured pattern of when and how often you send, including intervals between steps, daily send caps, and which days you send. "Frequency" is just the raw count of emails sent."
>
> **Source:** [link](https://instantly.ai/blog/email-sequence-timing-cadence-optimal-send-times-and-intervals/) · blog, 2026-03-16

> "Spacing follow-ups 3-7 days apart for the first half of a sequence, then extending to 7-14 days for later steps, keeps engagement high without triggering fatigue or spam signals."
>
> **Source:** [link](https://instantly.ai/blog/email-sequence-timing-cadence-optimal-send-times-and-intervals/) · blog, 2026-03-16

## Add value with every follow-up
A follow-up should give the prospect something new, such as a customer result, useful context, or another way to think about the problem. Instantly’s guidance is to make each message purposeful instead of sending one just to maintain activity.

> "The follow-ups shouldn’t simply repeat that message. Give the prospect something they didn’t have in the previous email. You could share a customer result, add useful context or approach the problem from another angle."
>
> **Source:** [link](https://instantly.ai/blog/cold-email-sequence/) · blog, 2026-01-19

> "Sending a follow-up just to send a follow-up is not a follow-up strategy at all."
>
> **Source:** [watch at 1:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xl0t4J9wtg8&t=103s) · youtube, 2025-06-08

## Persist with new angles
Silence does not necessarily mean rejection. Instantly teaches teams to re-surface at thoughtful intervals with a different angle, while paying attention to buying signals and signs that outreach is no longer productive.

> "Treat silence as neutral data, not personal judgment. Your job is to re-surface at smarter intervals with new angles until timing aligns."
>
> **Source:** [link](https://instantly.ai/blog/no-response-follow-ups-complete-guide/) · blog, 2026-01-31

> "The most persistent teams don’t just stack up messages; they mix formats and time their follow-ups to match real buying signals."
>
> **Source:** [link](https://instantly.ai/blog/how-many-touchpoints-before-a-sale/) · blog, 2025-06-20

## Stop when the prospect responds
Stop the automated sequence when someone replies or gives a clear no. Continuing after a reply, booking, purchase, or opt-out can make the outreach feel disconnected.

> "If someone replies or gives you a clear no, stop the automated sequence. Continuing to send follow-ups after a prospect has responded can quickly undermine an otherwise well-timed campaign."
>
> **Source:** [link](https://instantly.ai/blog/cold-email-sequence/) · blog, 2026-01-19

## Keep follow-ups in the same thread
For this feature, Instantly’s help article modified October 9, 2026 says to leave a follow-up’s subject blank to carry over the previous subject, or enter the same subject manually.

> "Leave the subject line of your follow-up steps empty to automatically carry over the subject line from the previous step, or manually enter the same subject line."
>
> **Source:** [link](https://help.instantly.ai/en/articles/7914807-keep-email-sequences-in-the-same-thread) · help, modified 2026-10-09
